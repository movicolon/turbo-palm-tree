A Statement of Want: Architecture as Infrastructure for Collective Life
I am looking for a home that refuses the premise of isolation—a living environment designed not as a fortress of privacy, but as an incubator for relation.
To dismantle the post-industrial household, changing our social mindset is insufficient if we remain trapped inside rectangular, hyper-compartmentalized boxes built for nuclear containment. I want a home defined by fluid, non-linear, and porous spatial designs.
Core Spatial Intentions
 * Soft and Permeable Boundaries: I want thresholds that dissolve the rigid division between rooms, allowing light, sound, and movement to bleed organically through the footprint. Doors and hallways should be replaced by shifts in elevation, texture, thresholds of timber and textile, and sweeping sightlines that connect rather than sever.
 * Centralized Communal Gravity: Instead of treating shared spaces as residual corridors or afterthoughts squeezed between private chambers, I want a home where the communal hearth anchors the entire plan. Kitchen, dining, and gathering zones must form an open, gravitational center that pulls inhabitants together by default rather than by scheduled effort.
 * Architecture of Cross-Generational Friction: The layout must actively invite unplanned encounters. By engineering overlapping zones and shared interfaces, the home should foster the organic friction of communal living—making care-taking, collective cooking, and shared rest a natural part of daily choreography rather than an administrative task.
 * Geometries of Interdependence: Moving away from the clinical separation of the traditional suburban grid, I want a porous architecture that integrates inside and outside, private rest and collective action, proving that a home can be a site of mutual aid, shared resilience, and extended kinship.
