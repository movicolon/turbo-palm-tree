# Tormenex

**Title:** The Jailer, Warden of the Outer Circles
**Rank:** Second Circle, Upper Tier
**Age:** Old enough to have built the first cage. He remembers the measurements.

---

## Appearance

Tormenex is built like the concept of restraint — broad, low-centered, difficult to push over. His skin is the color of raw iron before it rusts: a flat, silvery grey with none of the ember-glow that marks most devils. He runs cold.

His hands are his most notable feature: enormous, heavy, with fingers that are each about one joint too long. They are the hands of something designed to close around things and hold.

He wears an apron. Not always the same apron, but always an apron — heavy canvas reinforced with iron rivets. It has pockets, and the contents of the pockets change, but there is always a key ring, always a length of chain, and always a small notebook that he writes in with a very short pencil.

---

## History

Tormenex began as an enforcer for the Second Circle — the entity who showed up when a contract had been violated and the violator needed to understand the nature of consequences.

Over time, as the Soot Kingdoms accumulated more debts and more debtors, he took on administrative responsibility for the **Outer Circle Detention Facilities**: a series of vaults cut into the ash-rock at the edge of the First Circle's territory, used to hold devils who have violated the terms of their Compact obligations, goblins who have committed the handful of offenses serious enough to fall under devil jurisdiction, and occasionally beings from beyond the Cracking Shore whose paperwork is not in order.

He built most of the facilities himself. He knows every lock.

---

## Character

Tormenex is not cruel. He has thought about this carefully, because the nature of his work makes the question come up often, and he has concluded that cruelty is an emotional condition and he doesn't have it.

What he has is *thoroughness*. He believes that consequences should match violations precisely — not more, not less. When they match exactly, everyone involved comes away with a clear understanding of the system. This, he believes, is more humane than vagueness.

He keeps meticulous records in the small notebook. Length of detention, terms, date of release, condition upon release. He can be consulted on any case going back to when he started keeping the notebook, which was a very long time ago.

He does not enjoy his work. He does not dislike it either. He treats it the way a good mason treats a wall — something that should be done correctly because incorrectness has consequences.

## Relationships

- **Dusk-Elowen** — She provides historical precedent. He provides practical execution. They communicate efficiently and without warmth, which both of them prefer.
- **Malachar** — Receives orders from Malachar only in writing. Delivers reports only in writing. Has never had a conversation with the Archduke that lasted longer than four sentences. Considers this a working relationship in good standing.
- **Sniv** — The goblin chief has twice been held in Outer Circle facilities for Compact violations. Both times, Tormenex ensured the detention was precisely as long as specified and not a moment more. Sniv respects this, which among goblins constitutes a high form of regard.
- **Fern-Bite** — The young goblin has been caught trespassing in the outer vaults twice. Both times, Tormenex let them go with a written warning. He has not told anyone why.

---

## The Keys

Tormenex carries a ring of thirty-seven keys. Each one opens a specific lock in the Outer Circle facilities. He made all thirty-seven locks himself. No copy of any key exists.

If asked what happens if he loses the ring, he says he won't.

He is correct. He has carried the ring for over a century without losing it.

## Known Sayings

> "A cage is not a punishment. It is a *pause*. What you do with the pause is up to you."

> "I have never held anyone longer than their sentence. The sentence is the law. I am not the law. I am the interval."

> "The goblins are harder to hold than devils. Not because they're stronger. Because they're smaller than the gaps."
